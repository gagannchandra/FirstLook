At the triage desk

There is nothing to install and no account. Open the page on the computer or phone you already use.

  1. Type what you measured. Age, sex, how they arrived, the main complaint and six vital signs. If a reading wasn’t taken, tick “Not measured”.
  2. Read the chance of admission. Select “Check and save” and the estimate opens on its own page. A hundred dots show the same number for a quick glance across the room.
  3. See how sure it is, and why. “Likely admission”, “likely discharge” or “unsure”, with the readings that weighed most.
  4. Save it if you want to come back. Saved estimates stay in History on this device only.

Why the number can be trusted

It means what it says
When FirstLook says 30%, about 30 in 100 similar patients were admitted. Across the test, its percentages were within a point of what happened.
It admits doubt
Some patients could go either way. FirstLook marks them “unsure” rather than picking a side, so your judgement leads.
It shows its working
Each estimate lists the readings that pushed it up or down, and how strongly.
It needs no internet
Everything is worked out on the device. The page is locked against sending data, and measured traffic was zero bytes.
It copes with gaps
Missing readings barely change it. Even with every vital sign missing it stays close to its usual accuracy.
It leaves out who you are
Race, religion, language and insurance are never used. They were only used afterwards, to check it treats groups fairly.

It works with what your hospital has

Start with the basics. If you also have a triage level or the patient’s record, add them and the estimate gets sharper.

Common questions

More in About the project.

Does FirstLook replace the triage nurse?

No. It sits beside the nurse’s own assessment as a second opinion. The nurse decides.

Where does patient data go?

Nowhere. There is no account and no cloud. Saved estimates stay in this browser on this device, and you can clear them at any time.

Do I need internet?

Only to open it for the first time. After that it is saved on the device and works with the Wi-Fi off.

What if a vital sign wasn’t measured?

Tick “Not measured”. FirstLook was tested with missing readings and stays nearly as good.

Can I use it with real patients?

Not yet. It is a research prototype built on US hospital data and hasn’t been through a clinical study. Every patient in the app is invented.

          Why it exists

          Emergency departments are crowded. In the first few minutes a nurse decides who is seen first, and an early sense of who will need a bed helps the whole department plan.

          Many hospitals, especially in India, have no patient records system, no formal triage scale and unreliable internet. Most prediction tools assume all three. FirstLook was built for the hospital that has none of them: a nurse, a thermometer, a pulse oximeter and a cheap computer or phone.

          How it works

          1. The nurse enters what they measured. Age, sex, how the patient arrived, the main complaint, and six vital signs. A reading that wasn’t taken can be marked “Not measured”.
          2. FirstLook compares the patient with past ones. It has learned from over half a million earlier emergency visits which patterns tended to end in admission.
          3. It shows a chance of admission and how sure it is. Each estimate comes with the main reasons behind it, and is saved to History on this device.
          4. The nurse decides. FirstLook is a second opinion, never the decision.

          Reading an estimate

          The percentage

          “44%” means that of 100 past patients like this one, about 44 were admitted. The dots show the same thing at a glance.

          Low, medium, high

          Among patients marked low, at least 95 in 100 went home. Among patients marked high, at least 80 in 100 were admitted.

          The confidence check

          “Likely admission” or “likely discharge” means FirstLook is confident, and that check is right at least 9 times in 10. “Unsure” means the patient could go either way, so the nurse’s judgement matters most.

          What moved it

          The longest bars mattered most. Red pushed the chance up; blue pulled it down. These are patterns in past patients, not causes of illness.

          Three levels of detail

          FirstLook uses whatever the hospital has. Each estimate says which level it was based on.

            How it was built

            1. The data. 560,486 de-identified emergency visits from three US hospitals: a large teaching hospital, a community hospital and a stand-alone emergency department (Hong, Haimovich & Taylor, PLoS ONE 2018). Anything a nurse couldn’t know at triage was removed first, so FirstLook can’t cheat by peeking at the outcome.
            2. Choosing the method. Five kinds of prediction model were compared fairly on the same patients, at each level of detail, and the best for each level was kept.
            3. Making the numbers honest. The percentages were adjusted so that “30%” really means 30 in 100, and a confidence check was added that knows when to say “unsure”.
            4. Testing what goes wrong. FirstLook was moved to each hospital it hadn’t learned from, given patients with missing readings, and checked across age, sex and other groups.
            5. Making it small. The everyday model was shrunk to about 9 KB, smaller than a photo, so it runs inside a web browser on a cheap phone.
            6. The app. This page: one screen, live answers, works offline.

            Limits

            • It is a research prototype, not a medical device. It must not be used with real patients.
            • It learned from US hospitals. It has not yet been tested in Indian hospitals, where patients and practice differ.
            • It is less sharp for patients over 80, most of whom are admitted anyway.
            • It has not yet been tried by nurses in a timed study; that is the next step.
            • Every patient you can load in this app is invented for the demo.

            How the software is put together

            The web app is plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no outside libraries; a strict content-security policy blocks every connection except to this device, and a service worker caches it for offline use. The triage-only and triage-level models (logistic regression) run in the browser. The patient-record model (gradient-boosted trees, run with ONNX Runtime) runs on an optional local server bound to 127.0.0.1; start it with ./run.sh start web. Explanations are exact per-feature contributions (logistic regression) or TreeSHAP values (trees), on the log-odds scale. The confidence check is split conformal prediction at α = 0.10.
